# LearnForge learning-experience pilot

Status: protocol prepared; no participants recruited and no results recorded. This is a moderated discovery study, not proof that LearnForge teaches better than another app.

## Decision to inform

Can a learner understand the next action, explain why an answer needs work, recover from a mistake, and apply the idea in a fresh situation without losing their place? Does optional retrieval help them resume their goal rather than interrupt it?

## Participants and setup

Start with six willing adult participants across programming, an everyday nontechnical skill, and beginner language learning. Use anonymous participant IDs. Obtain consent before observing or recording; participants may skip a task or stop. Do not collect account credentials, medical details, private source files, or unrelated screen content. Use supplied fictional notes and test-provider accounts. Keep observation records locally; no automatic telemetry or sharing is part of this protocol.

For each subject, prepare two small, comparable concept sets, each with a plain-language objective and prerequisite assumptions. A domain reviewer must verify the content and grading rubric before sessions begin, especially translations and technical execution semantics. Do not use the teaching model's own answer as the sole reference standard.

If comparing LearnHarness, counterbalance both app order and topic set. Half use LearnForge/set A first, half LearnHarness/set B first; reverse the app/topic pairing in the second task. Match device, viewport, model/settings where possible, session duration, reference text and help availability. Record differences that cannot be controlled. Teaching the identical topic twice would favor the second exposure.

## Session script (about 25 minutes)

1. Ask what the participant expects to learn. Ask them to state prior experience without requiring a numerical self-rating. Give a short baseline application from the reviewed task set, without teaching its solution.
2. Create a topic from the supplied goal and notes. Ask the participant to describe the proposed starting point and what “keep for later” means. Check whether they mistakenly think placement establishes mastery.
3. Work through one explanation and one written or choice check. Let them describe what the app is asking; do not coach them toward the correct answer.
4. After feedback, ask: “What did you answer? What did the feedback say? What would you do next?” Note whether scrolling or navigation is needed to recover this information.
5. Present a genuine misconception or an uncertain grading example from the task pack. Observe retry, grading review and dispute. Check whether the participant understands that disputed or supported answers do not establish readiness.
6. Ask a side question requiring a small illustration. Ask the learner to explain what the drawing represents, what any control changes, and what the graphic does not establish.
7. Open the source notes during a question. Observe whether support labeling is understood. Switch away, restart and resume; verify answer/draft and lesson position recovery.
8. Simulate a provider failure with a test fixture. Ask the participant to recover without losing their response.
9. For an explicitly opted-in participant, offer one earlier concept as a revisit. Observe both “Not now” and starting a fresh application. Check whether they can return to the original lesson.
10. Ask one new, independently reviewed transfer question, without the notes or worked solution. Ask for a brief explanation of their answer.

On a separate later visit (for example 48 hours later), offer a new retrieval application from the same reviewed concept set. Arrange this with the participant during the study; this document does not schedule notifications or send messages.

## Record observable outcomes

Use the accompanying [session sheet](session-observations.csv). Record task completion without moderator help, time to identify the next action, loss of place/drafts, scrolling needed to recover feedback, assistance used, grading disputes and recovery success. Keep first answer, retry and fresh transfer outcomes separate.

Score explanations with the domain-reviewed rubric: 0 = relevant distinction absent or contradicted; 1 = partial distinction with a material gap; 2 = distinction applied correctly to this case. Record ambiguous responses as unresolved rather than forcing a score. Have a reviewer who did not moderate the session inspect anonymized responses where feasible. Compare their judgments and investigate disagreement with the model.

The delayed check should record both accuracy and explanation quality. A satisfied participant, a confident answer, a long response or a higher feature count is not a substitute for these observations.

## Review and product decisions

After each session, list concrete failures with the exact trigger and visible consequence. Do not average away a lost answer or an inaccessible control. Fix repeated usability failures, then rerun the same task procedure with a new matched case.

Report small-sample findings descriptively. Keep app version, model, subject, assistance and task set alongside each outcome. Do not publish a winner, retention uplift or placement-accuracy claim from this pilot. Decide whether a larger, pre-specified evaluation is warranted only after the flows are understandable and reliable.

## Before claiming the study has run

Replace this status only when consenting real participants have completed the protocol and actual observations exist. Synthetic fixtures, automated tests and agent walkthroughs remain software validation and must be labeled separately.
